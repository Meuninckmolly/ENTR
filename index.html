01 / An unexpected beginning

One detour.
A new direction.

After graduating from Baylor, Neil had a job lined up with IBM. During a six-month gap before starting, he began working alongside his father and learning the hotel business instead.

At 23, the appeal was ownership: the chance to build something of his own, with room to grow beyond a fixed ceiling.

“Whatever you get to create, and whatever you get to do, it's yours.”

Neil Ratanji · Interview transcript
02 / Spot the gap

Look at what’s missing.

In College Station, home to Texas A&M, Neil saw a hospitality market where the available product felt dated. In 2006, that gap suggested an opportunity: bring a fresher hotel offering to the market.

01 → +3

The interview describes a first location followed by three additional locations. This is the expansion sequence he discussed—not a claim about his current portfolio.

Entrepreneurship concept: opportunity recognition

Notice a customer or market need, then ask whether your business can meet it better. An opportunity is not just an idea; it starts with a gap worth solving.

03 / The value proposition

A room is a room.
Service is the difference.

Asked what value his business delivers, Neil returned to two fundamentals: a good product and good service. For him, hospitality is also about how a customer feels—and whether they trust the people serving them.

01

Read the guest

Some customers value personal connection; others prefer a fast, low-contact experience.

02

Adapt the service

Use reviews, surveys, and frontline feedback to understand what different guests need.

His point: there is no single customer experience that works for everyone. Technology changes expectations, so service has to stay flexible.

04 / Risk and trade-offs

Growth has a cost.

Neil described a hotel acquisition that did not go as planned. He had underestimated how difficult it would be to fix and turn around a property quickly. The financial obligation did not disappear while he worked through the problem.

He ultimately renovated the property from the inside out and returned it to the market as a Best Western. In the interview, he said it became his top-performing location in his portfolio.

“Go slow, but go wise.”

Neil Ratanji · Interview transcript

His lesson is not to avoid risk altogether. It is to understand the downside, protect your personal life, and grow at a pace your people and systems can support.

05 / The people behind the business

Build the team,
not just the footprint.

Looking back, Neil said he wished he had created clearer staffing structures and learned sooner how to treat employees well. He also emphasized humility: listen carefully to experienced people, stay open to what you do not know, and learn from failed decisions.
